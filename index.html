FIELD NOTE 01

History: patterns, people, power

A subject to keep revisiting

History is more than rulers and dates. It asks how ordinary lives were shaped by trade, belief, technology, climate, and political power. Looking across periods reveals recurring questions: who makes decisions, how societies adapt, and what people choose to remember.

Routes connect ideas

Trade routes carried languages, faiths, skills, and inventions as well as goods. The Silk Roads were a changing network of land and sea connections, not one road.

Sources need questions

A chronicle, coin, building, or personal letter tells a partial story. Asking who made a source and what it leaves out is part of reading carefully.

Then and now

Historical parallels can help, but no two moments are identical. Context lets us compare without forcing the present onto the past.

FIELD NOTE 02

Space: a moving neighbourhood

Earth is one of eight planets orbiting the Sun. Our solar system sits in the Milky Way’s Orion Arm, and the Sun carries its planets around the galaxy. One solar orbit around the Milky Way takes roughly 230 million years.

Science gives us ways to test ideas about this vast place. Telescopes collect light from distant objects; spacecraft bring instruments closer; careful models connect observations to explanations.

FIELD NOTE 03

Technology: tools and trade-offs

Technology changes what people can do and how they work together. To understand any tool, ask who it helps, what it costs, what information it needs, and who is responsible when it fails.

Computer science offers a language for building systems, but good design also depends on people: their needs, access, privacy, and ability to understand what a system is doing.

HISTORY · 4 MIN READ

Trade routes carried more than goods

The Silk Roads connected communities through trade, faiths, skills, and technologies. It is more accurate to picture a shifting web of routes than one fixed road.

Open reading questions

Which cities became meeting points? What made a route safe or profitable? How did geography change what travellers could carry?

SPACE · 3 MIN READ

Our solar system is a moving neighbourhood

The Sun, eight planets, moons, dwarf planets, and smaller bodies travel together through the Milky Way. Our familiar sky belongs to a much larger structure.

Open reading questions

How do astronomers define a planet? What can a telescope tell us from light alone? Which spacecraft are changing our picture?

READING · 5 MIN READ

Why the Underground Man argues with himself

In Notes from Underground, Dostoevsky gives us a narrator who is brilliant, defensive, and often contradictory. His voice challenges the idea that people always act in their own rational interest.

Open reading questions

When is the narrator honest with himself? Does contradiction make him less reliable, or more recognisably human?
